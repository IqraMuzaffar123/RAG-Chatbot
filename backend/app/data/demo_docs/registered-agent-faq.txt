# Registered Agent FAQ — Requirements and Responsibilities

## What Is a Registered Agent?

A registered agent (also called a statutory agent, resident agent, or agent for service of process) is a person or business entity designated to receive official legal and government documents on behalf of a business. Every LLC and corporation formed in the United States is required by state law to maintain a registered agent in the state where it is formed.

The registered agent serves as the official point of contact between the business and the state. They receive important documents including:

- Service of process (lawsuits and legal actions)
- Official state correspondence and notices
- Tax forms and notifications from the state
- Annual report reminders and compliance notices
- Other government filings and communications

## Who Can Be a Registered Agent?

Each state sets its own requirements for who can serve as a registered agent, but the general requirements are consistent across most states:

### Individual as Registered Agent

- Must be at least 18 years of age
- Must be a resident of the state where the business is registered
- Must have a physical street address in that state (P.O. Boxes are not acceptable)
- Must be available at the registered address during normal business hours to accept documents

### Business Entity as Registered Agent

- Must be authorized to conduct business in the state
- Must have a physical office address in the state
- Must maintain regular business hours at that address
- Professional registered agent services are available in every state

## Can You Be Your Own Registered Agent?

Yes, in most states you can serve as your own registered agent, provided you meet the state requirements. A member, manager, or employee of the LLC can be designated as the registered agent.

### Advantages of Being Your Own Registered Agent

- No annual registered agent service fees (typically $100 to $300 per year)
- Direct receipt of all legal and government documents
- Simplicity — no need to coordinate with a third party

### Disadvantages of Being Your Own Registered Agent

- Your personal name and address become part of the public record
- You must be available at the registered address during all normal business hours (typically 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday)
- If you are not available when service of process is attempted, it may result in a default judgment against your business
- If you move or change addresses, you must update the registered agent information with the state
- Not practical if you travel frequently or work from multiple locations

## State-Specific Registered Agent Requirements

### Texas
- The registered agent must have a physical address in Texas
- Can be an individual Texas resident or a business entity authorized to do business in Texas
- The registered agent information is included in the Certificate of Formation
- Changes to the registered agent can be filed online with the Texas Secretary of State for $15

### California
- California uses the term "agent for service of process"
- The agent must be a California resident or a corporation authorized to do business in California
- The agent's address is listed on the Statement of Information (Form LLC-12), which must be filed every two years
- The agent must maintain an address in California that is not a P.O. Box

### Delaware
- The registered agent must be located in the State of Delaware
- Delaware has a large industry of professional registered agent services due to the high volume of businesses incorporated there
- The registered agent's address must be in Delaware and is listed on the Certificate of Formation
- Many Delaware registered agent services also provide mail forwarding and compliance reminders

### New York
- New York uses the term "agent for service of process"
- The agent must have an address in New York
- The Secretary of State can also be designated as the agent for service of process, but a separate address for forwarding must still be provided
- New York requires LLCs to file a Biennial Statement every two years with current agent information

### Florida
- The registered agent must have a physical street address in Florida
- Can be an individual Florida resident or a business entity authorized to transact business in Florida
- The registered agent information is included in the Articles of Organization
- The registered agent must sign and accept the appointment on the formation document

## When to Use a Professional Registered Agent Service

Consider using a professional registered agent service if:

- You do not have a physical office in the state of formation
- You value privacy and do not want your personal address on public records
- You form your LLC in a state where you do not reside (foreign qualification scenarios)
- You want to ensure reliable receipt of legal documents
- You prefer to receive compliance reminders and deadline notifications
- You operate in multiple states and need a registered agent in each state

## Consequences of Not Maintaining a Registered Agent

Failing to maintain a registered agent can result in serious consequences:

- **Loss of good standing:** The state may revoke your LLC's good standing status
- **Administrative dissolution:** The state may administratively dissolve your LLC for failure to maintain a registered agent
- **Default judgments:** If a lawsuit is filed and the process server cannot locate your registered agent, the court may enter a default judgment against your business
- **Missed deadlines:** Without a registered agent to receive notices, you may miss filing deadlines for annual reports, tax returns, or other required documents
- **Penalties and fees:** Late filing penalties, reinstatement fees, and other costs can accumulate quickly
